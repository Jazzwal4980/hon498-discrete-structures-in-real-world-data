# Barrett College Fellows Fall 2026: Discrete Structures in Real-World Data using Python
Our project will explore how ideas from discrete mathematics, particularly graphs, sets, logic, and algorithms, can be used to understand and analyze real-world data. We will focus on network-based data, where relationships between people, organizations, locations, or other entities can be represented mathematically using graphs.

## Deadlines:
September 30: Group Report #1 due

October 2: First video assessment

October 25: Individual Report #2 due

## GROUP REPORT ONE - Graphs, SRGs, and Adjacency Matrices
### Part I: Getting to Know Graphs
Introduce the basic language of graph theory:

* What is a graph?
* Vertices and edges
* Directed and undirected graphs
* Weighted and unweighted graphs
* Degree
* Paths and connectivity
* Some important types of graphs
* Why graphs are useful for representing real-world relationships

Include examples and, whenever appropriate, figures, visualizations, or animations rather than explaining everything only in words.
### Part II: Strongly Regular Graphs
Dive deeper into strongly regular graphs and why they are useful and interesting.

* What makes a graph strongly regular?
* The parameters of an SRG
* Why SRGs are mathematically interesting
* Their importance in combinatorics, algebra, coding theory, networks, and related areas
* How SRGs differ from general graphs
* Famous examples such as the Petersen, Johnson, and Hamming graphs
* Other interesting SRGs that you discover

Try to emphasize why these examples are interesting rather than simply listing them.
### Part III: Adjacency Matrices
Explore the connection between graphs and matrices:

* What is an adjacency matrix?
* How does a graph translate into a matrix?
* What information can we read from an adjacency matrix?
* What changes when the graph is weighted?
* Why are adjacency matrices particularly interesting for SRGs?

Use Python/MATLAB/SAGE to construct some SRGs and their adjacency matrices. Compute and investigate their properties and look for patterns.
